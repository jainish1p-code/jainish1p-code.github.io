Should a scene generator focus on what matters for safety?

PA-D2iT studies whether a diffusion model should allocate its limited compute using scene meaning—such as pedestrians and cyclists—instead of visual texture.

Read the paper ↗Ask me about it

The question in one minute

Diffusion models can generate rare driving situations for training autonomous systems. But they cannot spend equal detail on every part of every image. A textured road or tree may attract a pixel-based signal, while a low-contrast pedestrian may be the region that matters most.

What changed

The importance map gives vulnerable road users a higher priority than vehicles and background regions. The comparison is controlled: every signal selects the same 25% budget, so the experiment changes where capacity goes—not how much compute is used.

Measured result

On the Cityscapes validation set, importance-based allocation identified vulnerable-road-user regions substantially better than entropy. It captured 78.2% of true pedestrian cells, compared with 34.5% for entropy and 25% for random selection.

Interpretation: importance achieved roughly 2.2× the F1 of entropy (0.541 vs. 0.247) while keeping the compute budget fixed. The result is about better targeting, not more computation.

What the paper also found

A pretrained Stable Diffusion 1.5 model with LoRA produced coherent layout-conditioned street scenes after 2,000–6,000 training steps. The from-scratch 7M-parameter SmallDiT was more appropriate for the available data than a 158.5M-parameter variant, which memorized instead of generalizing.

Limitations

The allocation study uses one dataset and one seed, and the current metric measures whether the allocator selects vulnerable-road-user cells—not whether a downstream detector or driving policy improves. The next stronger test is equal-frame-count training followed by small-pedestrian detection evaluation.
